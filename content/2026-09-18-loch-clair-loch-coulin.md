_A short walk on the south side of Glen Torridon taking in a loop around Lochs Clair and Coulin on a pleasant autumn morning before rain arrived_

<!--more-->

**Date: 18 September 2026  
Distance: 9.74km  
Ascent: 103m  
Time: 2.2hrs  
Hills:  None 
Weather: Blustery and cool with occasional light rain showes  
Route: [View on OS Maps](https://www.invertedworld.co.uk/hillwalking/hillwalk/558)**

The 10km or so walk around Loch Coulin and Loch Clair in the heart of Glen Torridon is well known as one of the most spectacular easy/level walks in the Highlands. Whereas most of the walks in this part of the world go up dramatic hills, this one loops around two lochs on the south side of the glen with those same hills forming a fantastic backdrop. It's a walk that's been on my radar for years and with a short weather window available before more rain was due to sweep in, I decided to drive north from Achnashellach to give it a go.

The drive around via Lochcarron, the bottom of the Bealach na Ba, through to Shieldaig and then along Glen Torridon was wonderful in its own right - roads that I hadn't been on for many years - and I stopped several times to take in the views. I arrived at the parking spot at the entrance to the Coulin Estate shortly before 10am. This should give me time to get the circuit done before heavy rain arrived around midday.

The parking was busy, even on a Friday morning out of the season, but I did get a spot and was soon on my way, with several other groups of walkers in sight, either in front or behind.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565605159/in/album-72177720335861174/" title="A&#x27; Ghairbhe"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565605159_6dad3b4221_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="A&#x27; Ghairbhe"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The walk along the east side of Loch Clair is along a tarmaced private estate road which makes for good progress. As I crossed the churning waters of A' Ghairbhe I got views up north to the grey slopes of Beinn Eighe, catching a bit of sunshine.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55564419487/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Beinn Eighe"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55564419487_f5e381a98a_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Beinn Eighe"/></a><figcaption>Sunshine catching the slopes of Beinn Eighe</figcaption></figure>

Beyond it Liathach came into view as I reached the end of Loch Clair. This is a classic photography viewpoint, recognisable from many photos, calendars and guidebooks. There was sunshine catching its quartzite slopes too although it seemed like the cloud was building back up.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565605659/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Liathach"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565605659_e377dd75ae_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Liathach"/></a><figcaption>Liathach</figcaption></figure>

I passed a couple of fellow walkers as the road wound its way below high banks of bracken, crowned by rowan and birch, turning a pleasant autumn hue. As I reached the far end of Loch Clair there were atmospheric views across the waters of the loch to Sgurr Dubh which sits on the south side of Glen Torridon.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55538425928/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Clair"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55538425928_e48e75e300_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Clair"/></a><figcaption>Looking over Loch Clair to Sgurr Dubh</figcaption></figure>

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55538318871/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Beinn Eighe"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55538318871_c1a08782d2_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Beinn Eighe"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

There was a bridge here that crossed the river linking the two lochs and led to the buildings at Coulin Lodge. This would be my return route later on.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55538703470/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="The track to Loch Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55538703470_b4784e1910_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="The track to Loch Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

It was nice to get onto a more gravelly landrover track that now followed the east bank of the river along to the end of Loch Coulin. Here the views once again opened out, although the cloud had now firmly filled in any gaps. After a short distance a narrow footpath left the landrover track, dropping down closer to the shore of the loch.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55538703650/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55538703650_bebefdb6c5_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

This footpath was well constructed and wound its way along the shore, sometimes close and sometimes further away to avoid boggy sections. It was a bit waterlogged in places but for the most part was excellent.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55564421547/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Sgurr Dubh"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55564421547_90ae51780b_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Sgurr Dubh"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

A littler further on, the views across the loch to the Coulin Hills had opened up and stopped here for a short snack break. It was wonderfully peaceful.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55538323516/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Torridon beyond Loch Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55538323516_66a3816afd_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Torridon beyond Loch Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

Beyond Loch Coulin the path crossed across some boggy ground to rejoin the landrover track near the [cottage of Torran-cuillin](https://www.beautifulholidayhomes.co.uk/accommodation/torran-cottage/) before skirting around the side of the garden. Here there was a herd of cows who I gave space to before finally joinig the track as it ran towards the big house at Coulin. This was nestled below the grey slopes of Beinn Liath Mhor which I had been up earlier in the week ([without any views sadly](https://tms.invertedworld.co.uk/blog/2026/09/beinn-liath-mhor/))

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565607584/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Beinn Liath Mhor"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565607584_b53e8cf170_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Beinn Liath Mhor"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

There were expansive views northwest over Loch Coulin to the cloud shrouded hills of Torridon. It is a fantastic place to be.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55538477629/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Torridon beyond Loch Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55538477629_8610c70303_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Torridon beyond Loch Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

Down by the river were a few more cattle, this time Highland Cows, which showed little interest in me as I passed by.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565607914/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Highland Coos"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565607914_b8eb0b2d3c_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Highland Coos"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565553213/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Highland Coo"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565553213_0876b36c2b_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Highland Coo"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The big house at Coulin looked well kept and beyond this the track now wound up and along the western shore of Loch Cuilin. 

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55537719042/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55537719042_0b9a857793_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

A light rain came on at this point and I briefly donned my waterproof jacket. It was shortlived though and soon a bit of bright sunshine appeared so I briefly left the track to drop down to the loch shore and take in the views of the cloud-capped Torridon hills beyond straggling pine trees.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565553518/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565553518_aa24ba05f2_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565553798/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565553798_885dead004_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

I passed a few folk now as the track gave easy walking with the light changing minute to minute as a few more squally showers blew through.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55564423182/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Coulin"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55564423182_f2a3400e42_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Coulin"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The landrover track left the loch behind and headed through denser trees to eventually reach the cluster of buildings around Coulin Lodge. A couple of estate workers passed in a 4x4 and around the well-kept cottages it was all very pleasant and civilised. Beyond the buildings I crossed the bridge I'd passed earlier to close the loop. 

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55564424557/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Clair"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55564424557_e9560a04ec_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Clair"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The views along Loch Clair were less impressive than earlier with the hills now lost in cloud that was lowering minute-by-minute with the forecast rain not far away. This though only served to enhance the beautiful autumn colours of the trees and bracken along the lochside road.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565856625/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Clair"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565856625_52db739033_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Clair"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

At the Liathach viewpoint I left the road to head down to the shore to get some alternative views out across Loch Clair. The wind had picked up and was whipping up the dark surface of the water. I lingered here for a few minutes.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565611564/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Clair"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565611564_a55045a516_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Clair"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565858000/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Loch Clair"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565858000_69eb9ecc81_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Loch Clair"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

From here it was just a short walk back along to the car. There were people just heading off and the car park was now full with people looking for suitable spots further down the road. As I reached the car the first few light spots of rain started to fall.

<figure><a href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/black_friction/55565858205/in/album-72177720335708331/" title="Beinn Eighe"><img src="https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/55565858205_d056f216ab_h.jpg" width="100%" alt="Beinn Eighe"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

I drove along to Kinlochwe and then up to Achnasheen where I stopped for soup and a coffee at the [Midge Bite Cafe](https://www.instagram.com/midgebitecafe/). This was doing a roaring trade and initially I sat outside before the staff offered me a spot indoors. This was lucky as the rain arrived, lashing down. The tomato soup was warming and delicious and I followed it up with a home baked flapjack that set me up for the long drive home by way of a petrol stop in Inverness.
